I’m playful by nature and a minimalist by habit, so I’m always chasing the balance: products that are easy to use and still have some personality.

How I build products.

Turn a vague brief into the real problem.

Early-stage briefs are often a line in Slack or a half-written PRD. I dig into users, the business and the constraints until the actual problem is clear.

Decide what better looks like.

We agree on the outcome before any design work starts, so we know what success means.

Bring engineering in early.

I share flows with devs before they’re final and build on shared components, so each feature ships faster than the last.

Prototype before anyone builds.

A working prototype shows devs exactly how it should move and behave, and lets users try it before anyone writes production code.

Keep improving after launch.

After launch I watch user sessions and metrics, and they decide what changes next.

Why hire me.

I move fast with AI

I use AI across research, exploring directions, copy and prototyping, which lets me test more ideas in less time. The calls stay mine: which problem matters, which flow feels right, and what’s ready for users.

Handoff that’s close to production

I studied computer science, so devs get a coded prototype, tokens that map straight onto their code, and every state covered: loading, empty, error, plus the AI ones like streaming, partial and failed responses. What ships matches the design.

I can run with a vague brief

Give me a rough brief and I’ll define the problem, agree on the goal, design it, work through the build with devs and keep improving it after launch. That’s how I worked as the founding designer at Goenkas and on Rex at Vudio.
