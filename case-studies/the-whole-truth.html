Five fixes to help mobile shoppers buy from The Whole Truth

The Whole Truth sells clean-label protein, and a quarter of its revenue comes from its own website. I audited the mobile product page for a first-time protein buyer and found five places where it makes buying harder than it should be. Each fix comes with what I’d measure to prove it worked.

Fix 01

Lead with the reason to buy

The problem

  1. 1

    Two weights in one title

    “15 g” is protein per scoop and “1 KG” is the pack size, so a first-time buyer can’t tell what they’re getting.

  2. 2

    Nothing but a title and a pack

    The first screen is the title and a big pack shot, nothing else.

  3. 3

    A faded thumbnail strip

    It repeats what the gallery dots already say and pushes the price below the first screen.

Why it hurts

Before the first scroll, the buyer learns what it’s called, not why it’s worth buying or how much they get.

What changed

  1. 1

    Title says exactly what it is

    Protein type added, and dividers separate protein per scoop from pack size.

  2. 2

    Hero shows the product, not just the pack

    Cocoa and a ready shaker sit beside the pack, showing the flavour and what it becomes.

  3. 3

    Faded thumbnail strip removed

    The gallery dots already signal more images. Dropping the strip pulls the price into the first screen.

Fix 02

One price. One clear number.

The problem

  1. 1

    EMI doesn’t read as a way to pay

    The EMI card is the loudest thing in the price block, but it looks like an ad, not an option to pay.

  2. 2

    Two signals, one discount, no connection

    The ₹530 OFF tag and the “code CLEANEST (auto-applied)” line sit apart, and nothing says they’re the same ₹530.

Why it hurts

The buyer pieces the price together at the moment they’re deciding, which adds doubt instead of confidence.

What changed

  1. 1

    Offers grouped in one box

    What’s already applied and how else to pay sit together, in plain words.

  2. 2

    Tax note folded into the price

    “Incl. of all taxes” sits under the price, one less line in the block.

  3. 3

    Per-kg price on each pack

    The 2 kg pack shows it’s cheaper per kg, so the bigger pack sells itself.

Fix 03

One control. Always with context.

The problem

  1. 1

    Same counter, twice

    Tap Add to cart and the button turns into a second counter under the first one, showing a different number.

  2. 2

    A sticky bar that hides the details

    Scroll down and a bar asks you to Add to cart or Buy now, with no price, pack or quantity.

Why it hurts

The buyer can’t tell what’s actually in the cart, and has to scroll back up to check what they’re buying.

What changed

  1. 1

    One button, then one counter

    A single Add to cart. Tap it and it splits into a counter and Go to cart.

  2. 2

    Sticky bar shows what you’re buying

    Price, pack and quantity sit beside a single Add to cart.

  3. 3

    Buy now moves to the cart

    No second purchase button asking for a choice the buyer doesn’t need yet.

Fix 04

Show the limit before, not after

The problem

  1. 1

    No limit shown upfront

    Nothing near the pack size or quantity mentions a limit, and the counter keeps letting you add more.

  2. 2

    The limit shows up only after you tap

    A snackbar says “Only 4 units of this product can be added per order.”

  3. 3

    The limit doesn’t match the counter

    “4 units” means 4 kg. With the 2 kg pack, 4 boxes still fail and only 2 go through.

Why it hurts

The page waits for the error, then explains it in a unit that doesn’t match the counter. Every failed try is a chance to give up or buy less.

What changed

  1. 1

    Limit shown before the first tap

    A quiet line under Add to cart states the cap upfront.

  2. 2

    The counter stops at the cap

    At the max, + greys out and the line says why. No error to recover from.

  3. 3

    The limit follows the pack size

    “Max 4 packs” for 1 kg, “Max 2 packs” for 2 kg. Same word as the pack cards.

Fix 05

Proof before the decision

The problem

  1. 1

    Rating below the buy button

    The 4.7★ rating sits below Add to cart.

Why it hurts

For a brand that sells trust, the proof shows up after the decision.

What changed

  1. 1

    Rating moves up, under the title

    4.7★ and the review count sit on the first screen. Tapping it jumps to Reviews.

  2. 2

    Recent demand, in one line

    “1K+ bought in past month” tells a first-time buyer others chose this recently.

How I’d measure

How I’d know it worked

It was never shipped, so there are no real numbers. This is the plan I’d hand to the growth team.

  1. 01

    Spot

    GA4 funnel: where do users drop?

  2. 02

    Understand

    Session recordings and heatmaps: why?

  3. 03

    Design & check

    Fix in Figma, review it, quick test with 5 users.

  4. 04

    Launch & watch

    A/B test or ship and monitor, based on risk.
