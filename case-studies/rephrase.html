Rephrase: Helping Freshers Communicate and Win the Corporate World

An AI tool for new joiners who find the corporate world intimidating. It turns informal thoughts into professional messages and helps them handle pressure, push back and stand their ground with confidence.

01 - The problem

Nobody teaches you how to talk at work

In my first job, I thought you just talk normally. Then I noticed the people who got taken seriously weren't saying different things. They were saying the same things, differently.

What I meant
What gets taken seriously
i'm drowning, i can't take more
→I don't have the bandwidth this week.
this is never getting done by friday
→Flagging a risk on Friday's deadline.
why is this my job?
→Can we align on who owns this?

So I said yes to everything

Extra work, impossible deadlines, tasks that weren't mine. Not because I agreed, but because I didn't know how to push back without sounding unprofessional.

02 - Who it's for

Built for people early in their career

They know their work but not the unwritten rules of how work talks. Most of it happens over chat, with people they've barely met. So they ask colleagues before replying to a manager, or paste messages into ChatGPT and get back something that doesn't sound like them.

03 - The solution

One screen, two jobs

Rephrase does two things. It translates what you'd say to a friend into what you can send at work. And it helps you handle the situation behind the message: pushing back, flagging a risk, or saying no without burning a bridge.

  1. Pick a situationOptional. Each one carries its own guidance, like framing a deadline as a risk.
  2. Sending toManager, peer, senior or client. This changes the shape of the message.
  3. How firmGentle, balanced or firm. This changes how strongly it's said.
  4. TranslateYour draft goes to the AI with all three choices.
  5. The messageReady to copy, with who it's for and how long it is.
  6. Why this worksNames the technique, so over time you need the tool less.

05 - Key decisions

What changed, and what I gave up for it

  1. 01How formal should it sound?

    First idea
    CasualFormal
    →
    Final
    ManagerPeerSeniorClient
    GentleBalancedFirm
    Noticed
    Nobody thinks "70% formal". They think "it's my manager, and I need to hold my ground". That's two questions, not one.
    Changed
    Split it into who it's for and how firm to be.
    Gave up
    Fine control. Three firmness levels instead of a smooth scale.

  2. 02What if they don't like the reply?

    First idea
    ‹2 / 5›
    →
    FinalRetry
    Noticed
    Comparing drafts confused more than it helped, and every version is another paid API call.
    Changed
    One Retry button. The new reply replaces the old one.
    Gave up
    Going back to an earlier version you liked better.

  3. 03Chat only, not email

    Noticed
    These conversations happen on Slack and Teams, not in long emails.
    Changed
    Every reply is chat length, ready to paste.
    Gave up
    Email support, for now.

06 - Design & theme

An open folder on a desk, not another AI dashboard

Most AI tools look the same: dark panels, glowing gradients, a sparkle in every corner. I wanted Rephrase to feel like sitting down at your desk with a file in front of you: your messy draft on the left page, the clean version on the right, and the clutter of a working day around it.

I designed it in Figma, then built it with Claude Code by describing each screen and checking every build against the design. I didn't write the code by hand, but every decision in it was mine.

08 - Testing

Tested on real messages, by real people

I tested Rephrase hands-on with 4 to 5 people in corporate jobs, from juniors to mid-level. They used it on messages they were actually about to send.

What testers flagged

Switching tone felt laggy, and some taps didn't register at all.

What I fixed

Every control now responds instantly, and every tap counts.

It stuckTesters kept using it after the test, whenever they're stuck on a tricky message.
